\section*{Summary}
Strong gravitational lensing maps dark matter substructure through arc distortions. We compare ResNet-18, LensPINN (physics-informed CNN with differentiable SIS lens inversion), and HEALSwin on DeepLense Model~I (30{,}000 train / 7{,}500 test; $64\times64$; classes: \texttt{no\_substructure}, \texttt{cdm\_subhalos}, \texttt{axion}). Phase~2 applies ADDA domain adaptation and classifies real SDSS cutouts (Cosmic Horseshoe, Cheshire Cat, Five-Image Quasar). Code: \url{https://github.com/Pallab9999/DeepLenseProject}.

\section{Method}
\textbf{ResNet-18} baseline uses spatial convolutions on raw pixels. \textbf{LensPINN} predicts Einstein radius $\theta_E$, inverts the lens equation $\beta=\theta-\alpha(\theta)$ with SIS deflection $\alpha(\theta)=\theta_E\,\theta/|\theta|$, reconstructs the source $I_{\mathrm{source}}$, and forms residual $I_{\mathrm{residual}}=I_{\mathrm{orig}}-I_{\mathrm{source}}$. A decoder classifies from original, source, and residual channels. \textbf{HEALSwin} maps inputs to HEALPix and uses Swin attention with a physics head. \textbf{ADDA} freezes source encoder $M_s$, trains target encoder $M_t$ adversarially against discriminator $D$ to align simulated and telescope feature spaces.

\section{Results (Phase 2: SDSS + ADDA)}
Real RGB cutouts from SDSS SkyServer were grayscale-converted, center-cropped, and rescaled to $64\times64$. ADDA aligned target features to simulations. LensPINN classified all three famous lenses as \texttt{no\_substructure} with $>99.9\%$ confidence---consistent with smooth macro-lenses at survey resolution (zero false-positive substructure detections).

\section*{Conclusions}
(1)~Embedding SIS ray-tracing in the forward pass beats a pure CNN on subhalo detection. (2)~ADDA plus coordinate-based SDSS ingestion enables sim-to-real inference. (3)~Extended training is required for transformer-based physics models. Future work: JWST/LSST resolution and multi-class domain adaptation.
